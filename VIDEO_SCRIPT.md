# Checkpoint video script (about 2 minutes)

Read at a normal pace. Each block is what's on screen + what to say.

---

**[0:00–0:10] Title slide: team name + members**

"Hi, we're [TEAM NAME]: [NAME], [NAME], [NAME] and [NAME]."

**[0:10–0:25] Dataset slide**

"We're using CMIP6 climate model data, specifically near-surface air temperature from NOAA's GFDL model. We use the historical run from 1850 and three future scenarios: low, middle and high emissions. We measure warming against the 1850-to-1900 average."

**[0:25–0:35] Graph 1: global mean by scenario**

"First, global warming. The scenarios split apart after about 2040. By the end of the century it's about 1.4 °C in the low scenario and 3.7 °C in the high one."

**[0:35–0:45] Graph 2: end-of-century map**

"But on a map, warming isn't even. The Arctic is dark red, the oceans warm less, and one patch of the North Atlantic barely warms at all."

**[0:45–0:55] Graph 3: warming by latitude**

"Plotting by latitude, the Arctic warms about 3 times the global average."

**[0:55–1:05] Graph 4: land vs ocean**

"Land also warms faster than ocean, and land is where people live."

**[1:05–1:13] Graph 5: latitude-by-year heatmap**

"This heatmap shows when it happens: the Arctic starts pulling ahead around the 2030s, and the gap keeps growing."

**[1:13–1:22] Graph 6: cities vs global**

"So the global number hides a lot. San Diego and Mumbai stay close to the global average, Phoenix runs about a degree hotter, and Svalbard warms nearly 7 °C."

**[1:22–1:30] Title slide: the question**

"That brings us to our question: Not Everywhere Warms Equally: how much hotter will *your* part of the world get by 2100?"

**[1:30–2:00] Screen recording of the D3 prototype**

(Press play, switch scenarios, hover a square, click one.)

"Here's our prototype in D3. The map shows warming for each region. The slider and play button move through time, and these buttons switch scenarios. Hovering shows how a spot compares to the global average, and clicking a spot charts it under all three scenarios. Next, we'll add a find-my-location button and polish the design. Thanks!"
